What changed since the first run

  • Identity questions are read from register fields. 44 answers (legal name, number, office, incorporation, parent, officers, status) now come straight from Companies House, GLEIF and SEC records, each cited to its record. Checked by hand, they are correct and specific: The Trade Desk's LEI record shows its 2024 move from Delaware to Nevada; Cvent's last SEC filing is a 15-12B deregistration from December 2016, when it went private. Before, retrieval answered "registered office" from privacy policies.
  • Corroboration is now a sentence, not a passage. One sentence must name the company and state the claim. 3 lines are corroborated and all 3 hold up on reading (PostHog product analytics and session replay, Progress's Agentic RAG); before, 4 of 11 did. The rule now misses one genuine case (Beamery's Skills Intelligence). A model check can be switched on to recover such misses; none was configured for this run.
  • Press releases are the company speaking. 24 press items were wire-service releases (Business Wire, PR Newswire and similar) and now count as the company's own voice, not as independent press. The 3 corroborations that remain rest on VentureBeat, Gigazine and Yahoo Finance. A release reposted by a finance portal without any wire marker (Yahoo carries many) is still counted as press; the rule catches those whose link or dateline names the wire.
  • SEC EDGAR now runs with a declared contact address, and resolved NVIDIA, Progress, Cvent and Brandwatch's US entity (Crimson Hexagon, which Brandwatch's site names as its registered US company).
  • A bug that mattered: rebuilding a company never removed sources taken out of its register. The wrong-company "STRIPE" record held back on the second run was still indexed and citable until this run. Removed sources are now retired from the index, for every knowledge area.

Still weak

  • Open questions. "What do users say", "how does it handle complaints" and the like are still answered by extracting sentences; about a third of those answers are on-topic. 61 of 170 questions were declined.
  • "Incorporated" means this legal entity. GLEIF's creation date is the date of the entity on record: Progress Software shows 2015, when it reincorporated in Delaware, not 1981.
  • "Not found" usually means the foundry could not see it. 52 of 173 map lines. Requstory reads page descriptions and menus built by JavaScript; the foundry crawls plain HTML.
  • Reviews: none. Trustpilot, G2, Product Hunt and Reddit refuse automated access; the App Store disallows it. User voices are Hacker News comments only.
  • Press licensing. Press items come from Bing News RSS, which permits personal, non-commercial use only. Production needs a licensed news feed.
  • Behaviour suite: 57/60. The three failures are one question ("employees in 1850") answered instead of declined.
